# HCF and LCM Questions

## 1. Larger number from HCF and LCM factors

### Original question

The HCF of two numbers is 30 and the other two factors of their LCM are 10 and 12. The larger number is:

### Tested concept

Expressing two numbers as the HCF multiplied by their stated co-factors.

### Approach

Under the intended aptitude-question interpretation, multiply the HCF by each of the two stated factors. The larger factor produces the larger number.

### Step-by-step calculation

Given:

$$
\text{HCF}=30, \qquad \text{factors}=10,12
$$

The two intended numbers are:

$$
30 \times 10 = 300
$$

and

$$
30 \times 12 = 360
$$

Therefore, the larger intended number is:

$$
\boxed{360}
$$

### Final answer

**360**, under the standard intended interpretation.

### Verification and wording note

Strictly, $\gcd(300,360)=60$, not 30, because 10 and 12 are not coprime. If the question requires the actual HCF to be exactly 30, its wording is inconsistent. The expected exam answer is obtained by multiplying the larger stated factor by 30.

### Mistake or trap to remember

When using HCF times co-factors, verify that the co-factors are coprime. If they are not, mention the inconsistency instead of presenting the assumed interpretation as an exact number-theory result.
